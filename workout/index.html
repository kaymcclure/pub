Knee-Friendly Strength Plan

Three one-hour gym sessions a week, with cycling and walking on the other days.

Your week

    Which weeks are you in?

    Knee rules

    • The pain rule. Rate knee discomfort 0–10 while you train. Up to 3/10 is fine if it settles by the next morning. Sharp pain, swelling, locking or the knee giving way means stop that exercise and use its swap.
    • Hips back, not knees forward. On squats and step-ups, sit back as if toward a chair and keep your knees in line with your second toe.
    • Control the lowering. Take about 3 seconds on the way down. Slow lowering builds strength with less joint stress.
    • Depth is earned. Only go as low as feels comfortable. Range of motion can grow over the weeks; weight is not worth knee pain.
    • Skipped on purpose: lunges, jumping, deep loaded squats and leg extensions with heavy weight. They load the front of the knee the most.

    When to add weight

    Use the two-for-two rule: when you can do the top of the rep range for every set with good form two sessions in a row, add the smallest weight step next time (usually 1–2.5 kg / 2.5–5 lb). After week 12, take an easy week with half the sets, then start again at weeks 5–8 with your new weights.

    Cycling and walking days

    • Bike setup protects your knees. At the bottom of the pedal stroke your knee should stay slightly bent. A seat that is too low is the most common cause of knee pain in cycle classes.
    • Monday and Friday classes come the day before a gym session. Ride them at a comfortable, steady effort so your legs are fresh for strength work the next day.
    • Stay seated on heavy climbs for the first few weeks, and keep cadence above about 60 rpm rather than grinding very heavy resistance.
    • Walks: 30–45 minutes at a pace where you can talk but not sing. Flat routes or gentle inclines; steep downhills are harder on knees than uphills.
    • Rest day: an easy stroll and some of the cool-down stretches are a great way to keep knees from stiffening up.

    This is a general plan, not medical advice. If you have a diagnosed knee condition, have had knee surgery, or pain is getting worse, check in with a doctor or physical therapist. They can tailor the exercises and swaps for your knees.
